There has been some discussion around AI being able to prove theorems that have haunt mathematicians for the last 100 years without a solution. Well, with AI, that moment has just arrived. 

The impromptu for this article comes from Raffaele Gaito's video "**do you trust an AI that you cannot understand?**" (in Italian, but with YT translation service, you can enjoy it for sure). 

We see AIs that are proving hard theorems: ChatGPT with Navier-Strokes, Claude with Reinmann, but the demonstrations are lenghty, probably unelegant, and not really understandable in 5 minutes. This is stuff that takes years to fully grasp. There are mathematicians that are working on the issue since forever, and they did little advances. Now, we see AIs tackling hard problem with ease. 

Gaito's video discussed about the two types of reactions that this kind of annoucements generate. 

- One type of reaction is **confusion:** if AI is doing all this work, are we - humans - still needed? what is our purpose here? 
- The other reaction is **rejection**: if I can't understand it, I cannot trust it. 

## In the next few years, AI will fix everything. Will we let it do it? 

Regarding the first reaction, **confusion**, I recently read another interesting article, "[Handing over humanity](https://www.writingruxandrabio.com/p/handing-over-humanity)". in the very near future we'll live in some kind of utopia where everything just works: an AI will decide how much money you have to pay in taxes, and those taxes are used exactly the way you think they should be used, or even better: to fund schools, to fix potholes in roads, to pay doctors, public transportations, garbage collection, climate crysis, war spending, immigration, and who knows what else. We'll reach a point where there'll be no need of politics and no need to discuss because, under any point of view, AI is right. Is humanity _designed_ for something like that? The author, and me, think that no: we humans love our freedom and to self-determine, which means, we want to be the source of our own happyness and, of course, problems. So, once we'll reach the Super Intelligence, what will happen to humanity? do we still have a place? I personally think that i'll organize barriqueades against the machines. 

## How we judge good vs bad 

Now, let's tackle the second reaction: **rejection**. This is probably the reaction I understand more. I understand it because, in every aspect of my life, I trust things that I understand how they work. Of course, I am not able to explain every possible detail. If you ask yourself why a sufficient amount of times, you may end with a post-doc in quantum physics, and of course we're not all PHDs here. Because at some point we stop asking why and we trust the answers. Sorry, not the answers: but the people that are giving those answers. We humans immediately understand who's capable of answering every possible question and those who cannot. Historically, we trust more those that can explain themselves. Those who knows their shit. I hire this kind of people. I drink beer with this kind of people. I also drink beers with people that say, "i don't know".  

So, will understanding the Reinmann hypotesis change my life? No. I'm not into mathematics, even though I may have liked be one of them. But, do I trust the person that has put the prompt in Claude to get the hypotesis proved? No. THIS GUY must show me that he has understood the proof. THIS GUY should spend his energy convincing the world how the proof works, and that the proof is right. He should have an answer for every counterexample, or objection. He has to do this not with me, but with his peers. I really don't care that Claude says so, I care that people that are familiar with the issue understand it and agree that the issue is solved. 

Looking at this with another angle: the best way to use AI right now is to understand new things. I do this all the time. I sometimes get very angry when I see that AI fails at explaining something. I once asked what is quantum computing, the answer was disappointing. I had to ask multiple questions to get something that barely clicked. How can I trust something I cannot explain to another human myself? 

For the moment, we should trust only what we really understand.
